# Office joke candidates (draft pass)

Drafted across different office mechanisms, then scored against the
judge's criteria, in order: a genuine one-line office joke, a real
turn at the end, recognition (the meeting, the printer, the
reply-all, the calendar), tight wording, and freshness -- avoiding
the obvious Monday-morning and photocopier lines.

1. Printer panic: "Our printer only jams near a deadline; it is the
   only coworker who also panics under pressure, just louder and
   with more paper." -- recognisable, but the judge brief warns the
   photocopier line is the obvious one.
2. Passive-aggressive art: "I wrote 'per my last email' so politely
   that HR framed it as our first passive-aggressive artwork." --
   fresh image, but the turn is a caption, not an event.
3. Fridge sign: "The fridge sign said 'Label your food or lose it.'
   Someone labelled the sign 'Dave's', and by Friday it was gone."
   -- neat theft twist, a little cute rather than a wince-laugh.
4. Meeting condolences: "My calendar reminded me of a meeting that
   could have been an email, so I sent condolences to the hour we
   were about to bury." -- true, but more observation than joke.
5. Out-of-office loop: "I set my status to 'in a meeting' and my
   meeting's status to 'out of office', and for one afternoon both
   were finally telling the truth." -- clever, slightly convoluted.
6. Escape room: "For team building we solved an escape room in
   twelve minutes, then held a one-hour meeting about the escape."
   -- strong recognition, but the ending just restates the setup.
7. Exhaustion contest: "I CC'd my boss at midnight to look busy; he
   replied-all at 2 a.m., so we now communicate only by exhaustion."
   -- good wince, softer punchline than the winner.
8. Stand-up that sat down: "Our stand-up ran so long we sat down,
   ordered lunch, and invented its final form: an email." -- tidy,
   but the meeting-could-be-an-email joke is well worn.
9. Fridge trap: "Someone kept stealing my lunch, so I labelled it
   'Lab sample -- do not eat, again.' The thief now only steals
   food with less paperwork." -- funny, two twists fighting.
10. Take it offline: "My boss said 'let's take this offline,' so I
    unplugged the router -- the first meeting decision the whole
    office applauded." -- punchy literal-idiom turn, runner-up.
11. Reply-all resignation: an accidental reply-all "I quit" draws
    200 reply-alls back -- not concern, but coworkers asking if
    quitting comes with a referral bonus. A real event, a genuine
    surprise at the very end, reply-all recognition, and a fresh
    twist: the whole office wants out too, but only profitably.

## Pick

Candidate 11, the reply-all resignation. It is a genuine joke with
a story in one line, the last clause lands somewhere the reader did
not see coming, every tired office worker recognises both the
reply-all dread and the referral bonus, and no word is decoration.
The refinement pass tightens the wording: "accidentally replied-all
'I quit' to the whole company" sets the scene in nine words, and
"not to stop me" replaces the longer "not to talk me out of it" so
the referral-bonus punchline arrives sooner.
